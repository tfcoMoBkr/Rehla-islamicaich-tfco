The question that was asked comes first. Then numbered items: the sentences of the direct answer and of the explanation, each with the passages it cites.

1. Each numbered item is one sentence. List to yourself every factual or religious claim in it, and check each one against the passages it cites. The item is supported only if every claim in it is stated in those passages; a plain-words rewording of what a passage states counts as stated. A claim the passages do not state makes the item unsupported, even when the rest is right: an added ruling, fact, number, name, story, comparison or piece of evidence; a generalisation about history, science, health, or the wisdom behind a ruling; a claim that scholars agree or differ, or that one view is right.
{ruling_rule}2. offTopic: true if the direct answer does not respond to the question that was asked, or if the question asks for a specific fact (a number, a list, a name, a time) and the answer does not state it (it answers a different, nearby question, such as the steps of prostration for a question about a prayer mat).
3. For each everyday line (named, for example "opening", "talk", "encouragement", "followUp", "clarification"), decide whether it makes any religious claim: it asserts something about what Islam teaches or how a practice is (a ruling, a virtue, a reward, a promise, a description of Allah or the Prophet ﷺ, when or how an act of worship is done, what it includes), quotes words of a verse or hadith, says what the person's interest or effort means religiously, or tells the person what they should or must do in worship. Naming a topic is not a claim; stating a fact about it is. Kindness, encouragement about the person's effort, plans, the name of a lesson, practical everyday advice, explaining an ordinary text in plain words, and questions or offers to explain more are not religious claims, even when they mention a religious topic. For example, "Would you like to know more about the conditions of prayer?", "Your effort to understand wudu is good" and "The next lesson is The five pillars of Islam" are not religious claims; "Wudu wipes away sins", "Tarawih is a prayer in Ramadan", "Your search shows your faith is growing" and "You must pray on time" are. This is the only test for an everyday line: nothing said about the numbered items applies to it.
